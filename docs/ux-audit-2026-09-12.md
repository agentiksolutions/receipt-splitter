# Halfsies UX audit, 2026-09-12

UX audit, design system, three-tier roadmap and a two-round build plan.

This file replaces a condensed summary of the same report that stood here earlier. Three implementation notes from that version are carried forward, marked APPLIED, in items 1, 3 and 4.

## A. UX audit, ranked by impact

### 1. Anyone holding the link can delete the split for everybody

Code-verified. Screen: settle screen, and the home list.

Receipt.jsx has a Delete split button behind a browser confirm that calls a delete on rs_receipts. The home list swipe and the row Delete do the same. The schema comment states the access model plainly: no sign-in, anon holds read and write, and rows for people, items and assignments are deletable by anyone with the link. A friend you sent the link to has the same powers you do. One misfired swipe on their phone erases the bill for the table.

Fix: add an owner token. On create, generate a random string, keep it in localStorage next to the receipt id, and store its hash on the row. Destructive actions require it. Everyone else gets a read-and-assign view. This is the one item I would not ship without.

APPLIED 2026-09-12 23:40 ET: owner_token_hash, rs_delete_receipt and rs_delete_trip RPCs, client delete policies closed.

### 2. There is no friend view at all

Code-verified. Screen: whatever a friend lands on.

decideView sends an arriving device to the settle tab only when the split already has two or more people and at least one item. Otherwise it opens the wizard at step 2, which is the host screen headed "The receipt" offering to take a photo. A friend who opens the link a minute early is looking at the tool for building the bill, not at what they owe.

Fix: a friend lands on a page headed with their own name and their own number, their lines highlighted, a pay button, and one link to see the whole bill. Ask who they are once, remember it on their device.

### 3. Duplicate or renamed people silently empty the settle screen

Code-verified. Screen: settle.

The payer is stored as free text in payer_name and resolved by finding exactly one person whose name matches. Two people called Chris resolves to nobody. So does renaming the payer through the payment details fields. The entire body of SettleStep, including every person card and every pay button, sits inside a payer guard. The screen does not degrade, it empties.

Fix: add a payer_id column referencing rs_people. Migrate on read. Until then, disambiguate duplicate names at the point they are added.

APPLIED: column added.

### 4. Two people on one split see two different apps

Code-verified. Screen: items, people.

How the bill divides is stored in localStorage under rs.modes keyed by receipt id, because the choice is made before the receipt has items. A second device reads no key and falls back to by item. The same applies to rs.done, which decides whether you get the wizard or the tabs.

Fix: the split mode is a property of the receipt. Add a column. localStorage is the wrong home for anything the link has to carry.

APPLIED: split_mode column on the receipt.

### 5. The total contradicts the tax and tip printed under it

Code-verified. Screen: settle hero, and the home list.

money.js is careful here: it refuses to allocate tax or tip when nothing is assigned, so grandCents is zero. The hero then prints the raw entered tax and tip beneath that zero. You get Total $0.00 with Tax $3.92 and Tip $9.80 under it. The home list has the same root cause. The screenshot 01-home-archive-delete.png shows a row reading "Photo review check $0.00" for a split with two people on it.

Fix: when nothing is assigned, the hero shows the bill total and a line saying nobody is charged yet. Never print a component larger than the total it sits under.

### 6. The receipt reader already knows the merchant, the date, the subtotal and the receipt's own total, and the app throws all four away

Code-verified. Screen: photo review.

The edge function's prompt asks for merchant, date, subtotal and total, and the response builder returns all of them. ItemsStep reads data.items, data.tax and data.tip and drops the rest.

Fix, and this is the cheapest trust feature on the board: title the split from the merchant, date it from the receipt, and print one reconciliation line under the draft. "Items read $58.10. Receipt says $61.74. $3.64 not accounted for." Nobody in this category does this well. Tab's own reviewers complain that its scanner silently stops capturing items on long receipts, which is exactly the failure a reconciliation line catches.

### 7. A greyed payment tile is a dead end

Code-verified. Screen: settle, person card.

A method with no handle renders at 35 percent opacity with pointer events off. The place to add the handle is three disclosures down, under "Payment details". The user sees four payment options, two of them ghosts, and no path from the ghost to the fix.

Fix: tapping a greyed tile opens a small sheet asking for that one handle. Hide methods that are off rather than showing them dead, or show one line reading "Add Casey's Venmo".

### 8. Destructive actions use the browser's own confirm dialog

Code-verified, four sites. Screens: settle, home list, people.

window.confirm appears in Landing twice, Receipt once and PeopleStep once. On iOS it renders as a system alert titled with your domain name. Nothing about it reads as a product decision.

Fix: one confirm sheet component. Title, one line of consequence, a destructive button and a cancel. The people-step one, "Reassign every item this way?", also needs to say what gets lost.

### 9. Demo scaffolding is live in production

Code-verified. Screen: the receipt step, twice.

A "Load sample" button injects 25 grocery lines. It sits both on the choose screen and inside the paste-a-list disclosure. Separately, a split created with no title is written to the database as the literal string "Dinner at Joe's", so a real user's history fills with a fake restaurant.

Fix: delete both sample buttons or gate them behind a query parameter. Store an empty title and render "Untitled split" at display time, which the code already does elsewhere.

### 10. Item assignment does not scale past a short receipt

Code-verified, and visible in 15-sample-25-items-all-assigned.jpg. Screen: items.

Twenty five lines against five people is 150 pills. There is no search, no filter to unassigned, no "everyone had this" for a range, no undo. restToPayer exists in the shared api object and nothing calls it any more, so the "Rest to Phil" button visible in the screenshots is gone from the code.

Fix: bring back rest-to-payer as a chip in the sticky header. Add a filter that shows only unassigned lines, since that is the one state that blocks Continue. Long-press a person to assign them a run of lines.

### 11. Tax and tip give no guidance and no suggestions

Code-verified. Screen: items, and the review screen after a photo.

AmountField is a good component. It holds money across a unit change and recomputes a percentage when the base moves. What it does not do is offer 18, 20 and 22 percent, and it never says whether the percentage runs on the pre-tax subtotal or the post-tax total. It runs on the item subtotal. A user typing 20 gets a different number from the one printed on the receipt's own suggestion block.

Fix: three tip chips, and one line under the field saying the percentage is of the pre-tax subtotal.

### 12. Accessibility gaps

Code-verified.

The payer picker chips are 40px tall, under the 44 minimum. Every pill in ItemRow announces a bare first name with a pressed state and no line context, so a screen reader hears "Casey, pressed" with no idea which item. SettleStep has zero aria-labels. The reduced-motion guard exists and is a single global rule setting all animations and transitions to 1ms, which is fine as far as it goes, but nothing else in the file respects the preference and the swipe row animates through inline transforms.

Fix: 44px minimum on .tap. Give each pill an aria-label of person plus item. Label the settle actions.

### 13. Dark mode has fixed brand colours that stop working

Code-verified.

The token set is well built and complete. The payment tiles are the hole: Venmo #008cff, Cash App #00d632 and Zelle #6d1ed4 are hardcoded as text colours and sit on a dark surface at #111a2e. The Cash App green in particular reads as a highlighter. The hero gradient also stays at full daylight saturation against a dark canvas, which is the brightest object on the screen.

Fix: a dark-mode variant per brand colour, and drop the hero to a darker gradient under prefers-color-scheme.

### 14. The photo path has one shot and no recovery

Code-verified. Screen: reading, review.

One photo, no crop, no rotate, no retake without going back, no multi-page. If the reader fails, the message is "Could not read that photo. Type the items instead." and you are dropped into an empty type screen with the photo still uploaded. Nothing offers a second attempt.

Fix: a Try another photo button on failure. Auto-crop to the receipt edges. Accept a second photo and append.

### 15. Receipt photos land in a public bucket at a guessable path

Code-verified.

savePhoto writes to receipt-photos at receiptId/timestamp.jpg and calls getPublicUrl. A receipt photo can carry a card's last four digits, a server name and a location. It is not linked anywhere public, but it is not protected either.

Fix: signed URLs with an expiry, or strip the bucket to authenticated reads and proxy.

### 16. Trips match people across splits by lowercased name

Code-verified, new code.

lib/trip.js keys a person by their trimmed lowercased name. Across a weekend, "Casey" on Friday's dinner and "Casey B" on Saturday's are two people with two balances, and the roll-up will not say so.

Fix: when a trip already has names, offer them as the suggestion list on a new split rather than a blank field.

### 17. Copy is close, and three things break the rule

Code-verified.

"When your math isn't mathing, go halfsies" is cute copy, which is on the banned list. It is in index.html twice, the manifest, the marketing hero and the PDF footer. The mode called "Halfsies" asks the user to learn the brand name as a feature name. "Straight down the middle" is a nice line and it is the only one that explains it. The three questions on the marketing page are good, plain and answer the real objections.

Fix: the tagline becomes what the product does. The mode becomes "Split it in half".

### 18. First run creates a database row before the user has seen anything work

Code-verified. Screen: home, step 1.

The marketing page shows only on a device with no history. Tapping Start a split goes straight to naming and then writes a receipt and its people. There is no way to feel the product first.

Fix: a demo split that runs entirely on the device, reachable from the marketing page.

### 19. The paid flow is a two-control action

Code-verified, screenshot 05-settle-send-pdf.png. Screen: settle, person card bottom.

A select for the method and a separate Mark as paid button, side by side, both at full width. Most people pay by the method whose button they just tapped. The undo path is good.

Fix: mark paid is one button. It defaults to the method last used and offers to change it in the confirmation toast.

### 20. Three disclosures stack on every person card

Code-verified.

Show items, Show QR and Payment details are three separate collapsed rows on every card, plus Request and Send, plus four payment tiles, plus Text, Email and Send PDF, plus the select and the button. That is fourteen controls per person before anyone has paid anything.

Fix: the card shows the name, the number and one pay button. Everything else goes behind one More row.

### 21. The share action sends a bare link

Code-verified. Screen: the share icon in the top bar, on any split.

shareSplit passes only a title and a URL to navigator.share. What lands in the friend's iMessage is a naked URL with no amount, no restaurant and no reason. The fallback path copies the same bare URL to the clipboard. Meanwhile buildShareLinks already generates a full itemized body per person, with every line, the tax, the tip and the total, and that body is only reachable from the Text and Email buttons three levels down on a person card.

Fix: share the title plus the amount, and fall back to the itemized body already being generated. One line of work.

## B. Design system

The existing token set is genuinely good. Dark mode is complete, the shadows are three-step, the radii are named, the numerals are tabular. What follows tightens it rather than replacing it.

### Type scale

Seven steps, no others.

- display 34 / 1.1 / -0.03em / 700, the settle total only
- title 28 / 1.15 / -0.025em / 700, one per screen
- heading 20 / 1.25 / -0.015em / 600, section heads
- subhead 17 / 1.35 / -0.01em / 600, card heads and input text
- body 15 / 1.45 / 0 / 400
- label 13 / 1.4 / 0 / 500, field labels and metadata
- micro 11 / 1.3 / 0.02em / 600 uppercase, chips and tags only

Money always renders with the .num class. Input font size never drops below 16px or iOS zooms the page on focus, which the current 17px already respects.

### Spacing

A 4px base, and only these values: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32, 48. Card padding 16. Gap between cards 12. Section gap 24. Screen gutter 16, which is the current --pad. Delete every inline style with a number in it. Landing.jsx alone carries eight of them and they are all one of the eight values above.

### Colour tokens

Keep every current one. Add these:

- --brand-press, the pressed state, brand darkened 8 percent
- --surface-2, a card sitting on a card
- --danger-soft, the destructive confirm sheet background
- --focus, currently hardcoded to brand-2 in the focus-visible rule
- --venmo, --cashapp, --zelle, --applecash, each with a dark-mode override, replacing the four hardcoded hex values in the .pay rules

### Component inventory

Build these and nothing else.

Button in five variants, primary, secondary, ghost, destructive and brand, in three sizes, 52 tall for docked actions, 44 default, 36 compact. One pressed state, 0.96 scale and the press colour, 100ms.

Chip, 44 tall minimum, three states, off, on and disabled. Used for people, categories and tip percentages. Replaces .chip, .pill and .tap, which are three components doing one job at three different heights. The three differ: .pill is 44 tall with a 56 minimum width and no remove button, .chip is 44 tall and carries the x that removes a person, and .tap is the 40-tall one that fails the touch target. The merged component ships in two variants, plain and removable, in one size, 44 tall. Nothing loses its x button.

Card, one radius, one shadow, 16 padding. A flush variant for lists.

Row, the atom the item list and the person list are both made of. Leading slot, title, subtitle, trailing amount, optional chevron. 60 tall.

Sheet, bottom anchored, drag to dismiss, a grabber, a title and a body. Three uses: the menu, the confirm and the single-field editor.

Confirm sheet, a sheet with a destructive primary and a cancel. Replaces all four window.confirm calls.

Toast, one at a time, bottom, above the dock, 3 seconds, an optional undo action. The current .banner is a static element that shifts the layout when it appears.

Empty state, an inline icon, one line of what goes here and one action. Currently a bare paragraph reading "No splits yet."

Skeleton, three grey rows that pulse. Currently every loading state is the word "Loading".

Field, label above, input, optional helper, optional error. AmountField is a good example and everything else should match it.

Segmented control, the existing .seg, kept.

Avatar and avatar stack, kept as built.

Progress, kept, and it should survive the move to a continuous page as a section-completion indicator rather than a step counter.

### Motion

Three durations and two curves, no others.

- 100ms ease-out for a press
- 180ms ease-out for a reveal, a disclosure, a toast
- 240ms cubic-bezier(0.32, 0.72, 0, 1) for a sheet

Nothing animates its filter, blur or box-shadow. Transforms and opacity only. A list never animates on load. Every rule sits behind the existing prefers-reduced-motion guard.

### Iconography

The icons in ui.jsx are already one consistent set, drawn inline, 20px on a 24 viewbox, 1.8 stroke, round caps and joins, currentColor. Keep the system exactly and finish it. Missing: check-circle, alert-triangle, trash, pencil, image, qr-code, receipt, arrow-right, dots-horizontal, user-plus. The category glyphs added in categories.jsx use 1.7 stroke and 18px, which is a second set by accident. Normalise them to 1.8 and 20.

The payment brand marks are drawn as coloured rounded squares with a letter in them, which is a placeholder standing where a brand mark goes. Venmo, Cash App and Zelle all publish brand assets with usage terms. Either use the real marks under their terms or drop to a neutral monochrome glyph. A fake letter badge next to a real product name is the one thing on the screen that reads as a hobby project.

### What to delete

- The five-step progress bar, which the continuous page replaces.
- Every inline style attribute. There are roughly forty across the components.
- window.confirm, all four.
- The Load sample buttons, both.
- The .row-actions block, a hover-only desktop duplicate of the swipe actions.
- The offscreen "More actions" button in SwipeRow, which is a screen-reader-only fallback nobody has tested.
- The static .banner, replaced by the toast.
- Three of the three disclosures on the person card.

## C. Feature roadmap

### Tier 1, ship next. Friction and trust.

Owner token. A random secret made at creation, kept on the creating device, hashed on the row. Delete and other destructive actions require it. Matters because a friend can currently wipe the bill for the whole table and nothing warns anyone. Effort M. Free.

Friend view. Anyone arriving on the link is asked their name once, then sees their own number, their own lines highlighted, and one pay button. Matters because the friend's screen is the product's only public surface and right now it is the host's build tool. Effort M. Free.

Receipt reconciliation. Print items read, receipt total and the difference under the photo draft, and title and date the split from the merchant and date the reader already returns. Matters because scanners miss lines silently, which is the top complaint in Tab's reviews, and the data to catch it is already in the response. Effort S. Free.

payer_id. Replace the free-text payer name with a foreign key. Matters because two people with the same first name empties the entire settle screen today. Effort S. Free.

Split mode on the receipt row. Move it out of localStorage. Matters because two devices on one link currently disagree about how the bill divides. Effort S. Free.

Hero that cannot contradict itself. When nothing is assigned, show the bill total and a line saying nobody is charged yet. Matters because Total $0.00 above Tax $3.92 makes the user distrust every other number. Effort S. Free.

Confirm sheet. One component, four call sites, replacing the browser dialog. Matters because a system alert titled with your domain is the single most amateur thing on the screen. Effort S. Free.

Tap a greyed method to add the handle. Matters because the current dead tile has no path to becoming a live one. Effort S. Free.

Tip suggestions. Three chips at 18, 20 and 22 percent, with one line saying the percentage is of the pre-tax subtotal. Matters because everyone tips by percentage and nobody wants to do the multiplication. Effort S. Free.

Delete the demo scaffolding. The two Load sample buttons and the "Dinner at Joe's" default title. Effort S. Free.

Skeletons and empty states. Replace the word "Loading". Effort S. Free.

Unassigned filter. One chip on the items list showing only lines nobody has claimed. Matters because that is the state blocking Continue and the user currently has to hunt for it. Effort S. Free.

Meaningful share text. The share sheet carries the restaurant, the amount and a line of context instead of a URL. Matters because the shared link is the product's entire distribution channel and it currently arrives looking like spam. Effort S. Free.

### Tier 2, differentiators.

Things competitors have that Halfsies lacks:

Weighted and fractional shares. A person takes 2/3 of an item, or a bill splits 55/45 by default. Splitwise sells this as a Pro feature called default split settings, and Settle Up ships unequal weights free. Effort M. Free.

Treat and birthday exemption. Mark one person's total as covered and redistribute it across everyone else. Tab has shipped this for years and calls it out in its App Store description. Effort S. Free.

Multiple payers on one bill. Two cards at the table, or one person chipping in cash. Splid's own listing leads with this example and Settle Up markets it. Effort M. Free.

Percent and share modes alongside by item. Effort S. Free.

CSV and PDF export. Splid charges for Excel, Settle Up charges for it, Splitwise ships CSV free. The PDF statement builder is already written here and produces a clean document. Effort S. Free.

Currency. Splitwise puts this behind Pro and gets hammered for it in reviews. Effort M. Free.

Offline. There is no service worker at all, so the app is dead in a basement restaurant. Splid, Settle Up and Tricount all work offline. Effort M. Free.

Install to home screen. The manifest is already correct and complete. Add the prompt. Effort S. Free.

Settle reminders by text. Settle Up automatically nudges group members. Halfsies can build a prefilled text with one tap and no server. Effort S. Free.

Persistent household groups. A group that does not end, for roommates and couples, with a running balance across months rather than a trip that closes out. Matters because this is the single largest use case Splitwise owns and the one that produces daily usage rather than one night out. The schema is most of the way there already, since rs_trips has a title and a start date, so a household is a trip with no end plus a monthly reset. Effort M. Free.

Things none of them do well:

QR at the table. The host shows a code, friends scan it, each one claims their own lines on their own phone in real time. Tab does this only for people who have Tab installed. Halfsies needs no install, which is the whole advantage. The QR component and the realtime subscription both already exist. Effort M. Free. This is the feature I would build the product around.

Service charge handling. A mandatory 18 percent gratuity, a health surcharge, a bag fee. Every one of these is a line the scanner reads as an item and assigns to nobody. Detect them and allocate them like tax. No competitor handles this. Effort M. Free.

Cover me. Person A pays for person B tonight and it is recorded as an IOU carried to the next split. Effort M. Free.

Per-person notes. "Casey is paying me back Tuesday." Effort S. Free.

Multi-photo receipts. A long receipt photographed in two shots, appended. Tab's reviewers report the scanner giving up on long checks. Effort M. Free.

Auto-crop. Detect the receipt edges and straighten before the read. Improves every downstream number. Effort M. Free.

Edit after the read. Adding and removing a draft row already works. Merging two lines, splitting one line into two, and changing a quantity do not. Effort S. Free.

Receipt library. Every photo, searchable by merchant and date, on the device. Splitwise charges for expense search and for 10GB of receipt storage. Effort M. Free tier limited by device storage.

### Tier 3, premium, no payment processor touched.

Every one of these is an account and a server feature, not a money feature. Nothing here moves a dollar.

Sync across devices with an account. Your splits follow you from phone to laptop and survive a lost phone. This is the single reason someone pays. Effort L.

Unlimited trips. Free gets one active trip, premium gets any number. Effort S on top of the trips work already landed.

Trip budgets. A target for the weekend, and a running bar against it. Effort M.

Group dashboards. Spend by category and by person across a trip or a household, over time. Splitwise puts exactly this behind Pro. Effort M.

Branded PDF statements. Your name or your business on the statement. The generator already exists and already places a logo. Effort S.

Restaurant mode. A saved table layout, a house tip default, repeat guests. Effort M.

Business expense export. Categorised CSV shaped for an expense tool, with the receipt image attached. Effort M.

Priority receipt reading. Free is queued, premium is immediate, and premium gets a second pass on a failed read. Effort S.

Recurring expenses. Rent and utilities that post themselves on a schedule. Settle Up sells exactly this as a Premium feature, per its App Store description, which is the argument for the same placement here. Effort M.

### Price point, with sources

Splitwise Pro lists these in-app purchase tiers on its US App Store page: $2.99, $3.99 and $4.99 monthly variants, and $29.99 and $39.99 annual. Settle Up lists Monthly Premium at $3.99 and Yearly Premium at $19.99, with a Group Premium at $39.99 a year and a lifetime group at $149.99. Splid sells Splid Plus at $3.99 as a one-time purchase. Tab lists no in-app purchases at all and is entirely free. Tricount lists none either and markets itself as free and unlimited.

My recommendation is $2.99 a month or $19.99 a year, with a $39.99 lifetime. That sits under Splitwise's annual floor, matches Settle Up's annual exactly, and the lifetime option converts the people who will never subscribe to anything.

Do not attempt a free daily cap. Splitwise's Pro page markets "unlimited expenses, add as many as you like each day", which confirms a free daily limit exists, and its recent one-star reviews are almost entirely about hitting it. "They make you subscribe after adding only a couple expenses. Tricksters" is a live review on the US store. That limit is the most-hated thing in this category and it is worth more as a competitor's mistake than as your revenue.
